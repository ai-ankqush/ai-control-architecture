For years we secured AI by securing the actor — who's allowed in. That's not the question anymore. The question is what the AI is allowed to do: which action, with what blast radius, and whether it can be taken back.

The industry never had a standard way to answer that, nor the technology to enforce it. Now there's both — an open, vendor-neutral framework, and a runtime that backs it. Which means the category is being defined right now.

I'm assembling twelve founding members to define it.

Kept deliberately small — this is about the quality of the people, not the numbers. Founding members shape the standard, are named as the people who set it, and each helps bring in the next layer of contributors. That's how this becomes a movement, not a document.

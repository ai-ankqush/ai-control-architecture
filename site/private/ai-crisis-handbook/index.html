Not another AI risk explainer

Most incident plans assume software does what it was written to do, data is just data, and a person is behind every action. AI breaks all three. This handbook tells boards, CISOs, legal, risk, communications and business leaders what to decide, in what order, and who decides it when an AI system takes a harmful action, leaks data, says something it shouldn't, is impersonated, changes silently or goes down.

First hour, day and week

Checklists you can run at 2am, starting with the six facts to establish: what the AI could see, decide and do, under whose authority, whether that authority is contained, and whether its actions can be rebuilt and reversed.

Contain, don't just kill

Eight containment options, from pausing actions to switching to manual, so you stop the harm without stopping the business.

Roles and decision rights

Who can contain, who can undo, who talks to regulators and who approves a restart. Fill it in before you need it.

Regulatory clocks

EU AI Act Article 73, EU and UK GDPR, NIS2, DORA and SEC Item 1.05, with what starts each clock and links to the sources.

Restart and board briefing

Restart criteria, including restarting with less authority and earning the rest back, and the five questions every board asks.

13-question readiness check

An honest score of where you stand today, and where to start.

Seven scenarios, with real cases

  • AAn AI agent takes a harmful action
  • BData leaks through an AI system
  • CYour AI tells people something false or binding
  • DDeepfake impersonation of your leaders
  • EA vendor's AI misbehaves
  • FThe model changed and nobody noticed
  • GYour model or AI provider becomes unavailable

Each scenario names the AI Control Architecture pillars that would have prevented it or limited the damage.
